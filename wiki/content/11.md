# Image Editor

A full raster editor for refining images.

## Features

- **Crop** — trim to a region or aspect ratio.
- **Resize** — change pixel dimensions.
- **Levels / Contrast / Saturation** — tonal and color adjustments.
- **Blur / Sharpen** — soften or enhance detail.
- **Shadow Remover** — GPU-accelerated shadow removal.
- **Paint** — freehand painting with a **Brush** or **Image Brush**.
- **AI** — paint a **Mask** and run a **ComfyUI** workflow on the selection.

## Seamless (tileable)

Makes a texture repeat without visible seams — the tool to reach for before using an image as bark, ground, fabric or any surface that tiles. Found under **Edit → Seamless (tileable)**.

Two different faults both show up as "seams", and they need different fixes:

- **The edges do not match.** Left butts against right and the content jumps. That is what **Method** fixes.
- **The lighting is uneven.** A photo is brighter on one side, so even with perfect edges the repeat reads as a grid of light and dark patches. That is what **Flatten lighting** fixes.

A texture often needs both. Fixing only the edges on a photo with a gradient is the usual disappointment: the seam goes and the tiling is still obvious.

### Methods

| Method | How it works | Use it for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cut** | Overlaps the image with itself and finds the path through the overlap where the two agree most, then joins along it | The default. Photos and natural texture — bark, rock, soil |
| **Blend** | Cross-fades the overlapping edges | Soft, low-detail textures. On detailed images you see both copies at once as a ghost |
| **Mirror** | Folds the image into mirrored quadrants | Noise and abstract grain. Tiling is perfect by construction, but the symmetry is obvious on anything with recognisable features |

**Overlap** is how much of the image the join is allowed to use. Bigger gives the cut more room to hide in, but more of the original is consumed — around 10–15% suits most textures. **Softness** feathers the join by a few pixels so it is not a hard staircase.

### The two previews

Both previews show the texture **tiled 2×2**, because a seam is invisible on a single image and unmistakable the moment it repeats. Judge the result there, not on the main canvas.

Each is labelled *invisible*, *faint*, *visible* or *obvious*. That is measured, not decorative: it compares the difference across the tile boundary against the image's own grain. A texture whose edges differ no more than neighbouring pixels inside it has, by definition, no seam to see.

### Size

**Keep original size** is on by default, so a 1024×1024 texture stays 1024×1024 — which is what an engine wants. Joining consumes some pixels, so the image is enlarged slightly first to compensate.

Turn it off and the image is cropped instead, coming out smaller with no resampling at all. That is a **document** change: every layer and the mask are resized with it, exactly as the Resize tool does.

> Do this **before** upscaling or sharpening. Anything that resamples a finished tile blends its outermost pixels with themselves rather than with the pixels they wrap to, which quietly re-opens the seam.
