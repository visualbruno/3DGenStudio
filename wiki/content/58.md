# Update

Runs **every frame, for every living particle**. Forces, over-life changes, collision.

This is where an effect gets its character. Initialize decides where particles start; Update decides what happens to them.

## Forces

| Block | Properties | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Add Gravity** | `gravity` ([0, -9.8, 0]) | A constant acceleration. Point it **up** for buoyancy — that is how fire and smoke rise. |
| **Add Drag** | `drag` (1.5) | Air resistance. This is what makes a burst decelerate rather than fly off at a constant speed — the difference between sparks and bullets. |
| **Add Turbulence** | `strength` (1), `frequency` (0.6) | Swirling noise. `frequency` is the scale of the swirls: low is a slow drift, high is a jitter. |
| **Attract to Point** | `position`, `strength` (6), `radius` (2) | Pulls particles toward a point. Gathering magic, swarms. |
| **Vortex** | `position`, `axis`, `strength` (4), `inward` (1) | Spins particles around an axis. `inward` also draws them toward it — portals and whirlwinds. |
| **Speed Limit** | `speed` (10) | Caps velocity. A safety net: an attractor or a vortex can otherwise accelerate particles indefinitely. |

**Drag is the most under-used block here.** Almost anything that looks slightly wrong — a burst that flies too far, smoke that rises too fast, a spray that never settles — improves with a little drag.

## Over life

| Block | Properties | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Size Over Life** | `scale` (1) | A multiplier on the starting size. Set it to a curve. |
| **Colour Over Life** | `color` | Set it to a gradient. Fading the **alpha** to 0 at the end is what stops particles popping out of existence. |
| **Spin** | `speed` (45) | Degrees per second. Random between two values makes tumbling debris. |

These read the particle's **normalized age** — 0 at birth, 1 at death — so they depend directly on Set Lifetime.

Nearly every convincing effect has both of these. Something that appears, changes and fades reads as alive; something that appears and vanishes reads as a bug.

## Collision

| Block | Properties | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Collide with Floor** | `height` (0), `bounce` (0.3), `friction` (0.2) | A horizontal plane. |
| **Collide with Sphere** | `position`, `radius`, `bounce`, `friction` + side | `outside` keeps particles out, `inside` keeps them in. |
| **Collide with Box** | `position`, `size`, `bounce`, `friction` + side | Same, for a box. |

High **friction** makes debris settle instead of sliding forever. That is usually what you want for anything that should come to rest.

## Housekeeping

| Block | Properties | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Kill Outside Box** | `size` ([20, 20, 20]) | Removes particles that leave the volume. |
| **Play Sprite Sheet** | `frames` (16), `rate` (24) + timing mode | Animates a flipbook. `life` plays the sheet exactly once however long the particle lives — right for an explosion. `rate` plays at a fixed speed and loops — right for a torch. |

**Kill Outside Box is how weather is made affordable.** A raindrop that has fallen past the floor costs exactly as much as one you can see until something removes it. Bounding the volume is cheaper than raising the capacity.

**Play Sprite Sheet** needs a matching **Sprite Sheet** block in the Output stage saying how the texture is divided. One without the other does nothing.

## Order matters

Blocks run top to bottom. Two things follow from that:

- A **Set Size** style block below a **Size Over Life** would overwrite it every frame.
- Collision runs *after* the forces in the same stack, and the integrator that moves particles runs after everything. This is why a collision that seems to let particles sink slightly is normally a bounce value near zero rather than a broken plane.

## In the engines

Most of this is native in Unity: gravity, drag, size and colour over life, spin, speed limit and floor collision all have direct equivalents.

Four carry a caveat, reported per import:

- **Add Turbulence** becomes Unity's noise module — value noise rather than the curl noise used here, so the motion differs in character.
- **Vortex** becomes orbital velocity: the swirl is right, but it is a fixed angular rate rather than a force, so it does not fall off with distance.
- **Collide with Sphere / Box** have no implicit-shape equivalent; Unity collides against scene colliders or planes.
- **Attract to Point** and **Kill Outside Box** have no equivalent at all and are dropped, named in the report.

Set **Target** in the toolbar to see these as warnings while you author, rather than discovering them at import.
