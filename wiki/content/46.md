# Custom Animations

The **Custom** tab (in the Mesh Editor's Auto Rig side panel, next to **[MoCap](45.md)**) holds the animations *you* made: clips you corrected by hand in the **animation editor** and saved, so the work outlives the session and can be put on any other rigged mesh.

Where **[Animations](34.md)** gives you the bundled reference library, **[Kimodo](43.md)** generates motion from a prompt and **[MoCap](45.md)** captures it from a video, this tab replays your own edits.

## Saving an animation

1. Play any clip on your mesh (from **[Animations](34.md)**, **[Kimodo](43.md)** or **[MoCap](45.md)**).
2. Click **Edit animation** to open the animation editor dock under the viewport, and correct whatever needs correcting.
3. Click **Save animation** in the dock's header, give it a name, confirm.

What gets stored is the clip **and the skeleton it was authored on**. Both halves are needed: retargeting measures every frame as a change from the source rig's rest pose, so a clip on its own could only ever be replayed on the exact mesh it came from. With its skeleton travelling with it, your edit becomes a proper animation *source*.

## Importing animations you already own

Animations bought on a marketplace (Unity Asset Store, ActorCore…), downloaded from Mixamo, or exported from Blender can be brought straight in — **FBX**, **GLB/glTF** and **BVH**.

1. Open the **Custom** tab and click **Select animations**.
2. Click **Import file…** and pick one or many files at once. A Mixamo download is one clip per file; a pack is often dozens of clips in one.
3. The file is *read* first and its clips listed. Untick anything you do not want, then click **Import**.

What gets stored is each clip **plus the skeleton the file carries** — that is what lets a clip authored for someone else's character be retargeted onto yours. Bones are matched by name where the names agree (a Mixamo-named pack onto a Mixamo-named rig maps itself), and anything else is one pass through **[Bone Mapping](35.md)**, saved with your mesh so it is never repeated.

> If an imported motion looks offset rather than wrong, toggle **Match reference rest pose**. It decides whether the clip is measured against the file's own rest pose or your mesh's stance.

Scale tracks are dropped on import, and so is anything that does not address a bone: the retargeter reads the source skeleton's world transforms every frame, and an animated scale would stretch the very thing it is measuring.

## Applying one to a mesh

1. Open the **Custom** tab, click **Select animations**, and pick one or several in the library popup (search by name or source file; the play button on a row applies just that one).
2. If the animation was made on a rig with the same bone names as your mesh — the usual case when both came out of **[Auto Rig](32.md)** — the bones map themselves and the clip starts playing straight away.
3. Otherwise, click **Map bones** and match the two skeletons in the **[Bone Mapping](35.md)** dialog. The mapping is saved with the mesh, so this is a one-time step.
4. Preview, tick the clips you want, and click **Save mesh with N animations** to embed them as a new version — exactly as on the **[Animations](34.md)** tab.

Applying more animations made on the **same rig** adds them beside the first and keeps the mapping. One made on a *different* rig replaces the source, the same way picking another reference mesh does — the Auto Rig panel has a single source-rig slot shared by all five animation tabs. That is also why a selection spanning two different rigs cannot all be applied at once: the first one is applied and the rest are named back to you.

## Editing further

An applied custom animation is an ordinary clip on your mesh: open the animation editor again, correct it, and save it as another animation. The original stays as it was.

## Managing the library

| Button | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Search** | Filter by animation name or by the file / mesh it came from. |
| **Rename** | Rename the saved animation. |
| **Delete** | Remove it for good (click twice to confirm). A clip already applied to the mesh keeps playing until you leave the page. |

Custom animations are **global**, not per-project: an animation saved off one character is available on every mesh in every project.
